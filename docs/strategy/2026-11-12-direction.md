# Fix the Web direction and website proposal

Prepared for Nicholas Nucifora on 12 November 2026. This is a strategy and content proposal; it does not change the website.

Implementation update: following Nicholas's subsequent request, a first local preview now implements the homepage, Villow case study, design/access brief and full submission. The Den and Life calculator are retained under `/old-pages` for later review. Production remains the coming-soon page. The proposal below records the reasoning for this first version.

Fix the Web should bring people together around a practical ambition: digital services that better serve the people using them. Villow is the first substantial expression of that ambition. Its value is both the alternative people can experience and the design questions it makes possible to investigate.

The strongest direction combines conviction, building and honest learning. Keep the broad name and the invitation to improve the web. Concentrate the current work on Villow, explain the reasoning behind a small number of its design choices, and build evidence about their effects. Use that experience to make specific proposals for platforms and policy. There is room for wider ideas without promising to build or research them all.

## The central argument

Social media is useful. People have good reasons to learn, relax, discover things, follow creators and spend time with friends through it. That usefulness is part of why its problems are difficult to escape: leaving can also mean giving up things people value. Your latest Villow personal message expresses this especially well.

Fix the Web can make a positive demand from there: services should help people retain those benefits while giving them meaningful choices about how the experience works. A person should be able to enjoy watching, decide what matters to them, understand their patterns and stop when they want to.

That position reaches beyond a target number of hours. Entertainment needs no productivity justification. More time online is not automatically failure, and less time is not automatically success. Personal agency is also only part of the wider problem: deliberate choices can still expose others to abuse or spread misleading information. Those issues deserve attention on their own terms.

The public identity can remain an independent, grassroots project with movement ambitions. Describe the actual people and work plainly. An established laboratory, academic partnership, membership organisation or research programme should become part of the description when it exists.

**Working mission:** Help digital services work better for the people using them.

**Present work:** Build and explain alternative designs through Villow, learn from people using them, and develop specific proposals for improvement.

**Longer ambition:** Improve the services people already use by turning useful findings into changes platforms can adopt and proposals policymakers can consider. Make those lessons available to others and help create the conditions for independent alternatives to be built and evaluated.

## What the Villow previews add

The previews make the direction substantially more concrete than the three attached documents alone.

First, the previews present different preferences as legitimate. The Shorts fan, curious browser, channel gardener, evening planner and all-rounder stories show different ways to use Villow. The app, extension and combined setup routes offer several ways into the experience. Fix the Web should preserve this pluralism: it is developing ways people can shape their experience, rather than identifying one approved way to use YouTube.

Second, reflection leads to action. The worth-it question, viewing patterns and proposed channel adjustments form a useful sequence: notice something, understand it, choose whether to change it. Granular subscription controls matter because a following feed must be useful and manageable if it is to be a real alternative.

Third, friendship is a positive part of the design. Personal recommendations and shared viewing are valuable experiences to investigate in their own right. They should not be reduced to instruments for enforcing limits. Accountability is a separate social mechanism, with different questions about pressure and privacy.

Fourth, the design concepts show a wider interest in the quality of online life: corrections, creator actions, meaningful recommendations, AI assistance and authentic discussion. These deserve a visible place as proposals. They do not establish that Villow currently reduces misinformation, improves democracy or solves botting.

Finally, setup and hosting expose the conditions under which alternatives are possible. Account setup, access permissions, quotas, hosting costs and responsibility for friends' data are part of the practical experience. This is valuable material for Fix the Web's access argument, but not a reason to import a hosting manual into its homepage.

## The role of each website

| Villow | Fix the Web |
| --- | --- |
| Help someone understand, choose and use the product. | Explain why the work matters and what can be learned from it. |
| Show features, everyday scenarios and app versus extension choices. | Connect a few examples to broader design questions. |
| Own setup, hosting, support and product-specific proposals. | Own the wider mission, evaluation reports and access-policy argument. |
| Invite product feedback and practical contributions. | Invite research, design, technical and policy collaboration around defined work. |

Both sites can link to the same project and people. Avoid copying the full product tour onto Fix the Web or maintaining two diverging explanations of every feature. Keep YouTube-specific concepts on Villow initially; Fix the Web can introduce the questions and link to the detailed examples. A later cross-platform investigation can have its own canonical Fix the Web article.

The Villow handoff also identifies existing support infrastructure that can span both projects. Reuse one clearly explained support relationship when ready, with its existing consent and privacy boundaries, rather than creating a competing donation system. Ordinary participation should remain useful without a payment or a hosted installation.

Someone arriving from Villow should learn something new: what the broader effort is, what is known, what needs testing and how the lessons could travel. Someone arriving from a policy discussion should reach a tangible example quickly.

## Scope that preserves the ambition

Use these questions to organise the work internally and to choose examples. They need not become separate website departments.

| Question | Concrete starting point | Immediate next step |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Can people choose when to continue and when to finish? | Finite feeds, playback choices, selected boundaries and stopping prompts. | Understand whether people can reach wanted content and finish as intended. |
| Can people understand and shape their experience? | Worth-it reflection, viewing information, subscription controls and suggestions. | Test comprehension, usefulness and the effort required to make changes. |
| How can online experiences support relationships people value? | Personal recommendations and watching together; separately, optional accountability. | Learn what feels connecting, demanding, helpful or intrusive. |
| What would improve trust and information quality? | Community notes, correction notifications, meaningful recommendations and AI-assisted review concepts. | Develop a small number of proposals with appropriate evidence and specialist input. |
| What access makes independent evaluation possible? | The capabilities and constraints encountered while building Villow. | Record specific needs and constraints, then formulate a proportionate access proposal. |

The first three connect directly to the experience described in the product previews. Information integrity is a concepts agenda at this stage. Access enables the whole effort and is an appropriate early policy priority.

Cross-ecosystem wellbeing belongs in one bounded proposal. Apple already offers combined Screen Time reporting across its devices, so the useful question is broader compatibility, understandable measurement and voluntary sharing—not whether any cross-device reporting exists. [Apple Screen Time guide](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/set-up-screen-time-for-yourself-iphbfa595995/ios)

Gaming, Discord, AI companions and other environments can be acknowledged as future applications. Start substantial work in another environment when there is a specific question, a workable way to investigate it, relevant expertise, a clear output and capacity to maintain the existing work. This leaves the ambition open without treating every concern as a current programme.

## What confidence should rest on

Separate what exists from what has been established about it.

| Implementation | Evaluation |
| --- | --- |
| Proposal, demonstration, functioning feature, available product. | Not evaluated, formative feedback, observational findings, comparative evidence, independent evaluation. |

A polished demo can explain an idea without testing its effects. A functioning feature can still need evaluation. A proposal can draw on external research without that research validating its particular implementation. Independence from a platform is also different from evaluation independent of the product's creator.

The homepage needs only a clear statement that Villow is a working alternative whose design ideas still need evaluation. Detailed work pages should give the version, question, method, participants or evidence source, limitations and next decision. Use plain phrases in context; the site does not need a wall of badges.

The principle that people should have meaningful choice is a value commitment. It does not depend on first proving a wellbeing effect. Claims that a particular prompt, streak or feed improves an outcome do require evidence. This distinction allows Fix the Web to speak firmly about its purpose and carefully about its findings.

A useful publication promise is: **share what we tried, what happened and what should change.** This includes unhelpful results and ideas that are abandoned. Product uptake and positive testimonials can be useful signals, but they are not substitutes for evidence of an intervention's effect.

## A manageable first evaluation programme

Begin with understanding the overall experience. Can newcomers find videos they want, choose a suitable route, configure the essential settings and recover from a choice that feels too restrictive? Include people who stop using it; enthusiastic continuing users alone will miss important problems.

Then select a small number of questions for more focused work. The following are proposed investigations, not studies already underway.

| Design question | What to observe or ask | What could change the design |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Does a finite feed create a useful sense of completion? | Whether people find wanted content, recognise the ending and feel able to finish; whether they immediately seek more elsewhere. | Confusion, missing valued content or unwanted substitution may point to different boundaries or clearer choices. |
| Does worth-it reflection help people understand their viewing? | Whether the question is understood, voluntarily used and informative over time; skipped ratings, annoyance and guilt. | A high burden or misleading interpretation may justify different timing, wording or frequency. |
| Are subscription controls usable enough to be a real alternative? | Whether people can obtain the feed they want without excessive configuration; whether they understand and can reverse suggestions. | Configuration effort may call for simpler defaults and guided adjustments. |

For later comparative studies, define the outcome and comparison before interpreting changes. Whole-product feedback helps improve Villow, but cannot isolate the contribution of every feature. A researcher can help choose feasible methods rather than adding measurement indiscriminately.

Use satisfaction, perceived control, unwanted continuation, effort, ease of stopping and retained benefits alongside usage totals. Ask proportionately about displacement into other services; a complete record of every device is not necessary for an initial investigation. Missing or skipped worth-it ratings should remain visible when presenting any aggregate.

Existing personal statistics are not automatically a research dataset. A study needs a defined collection purpose and participant understanding. Hosting by a friend also creates a practical data-access relationship that must be understood before claims about privacy or distributed research are made.

## Tensions worth investigating openly

**Commitment and changing one's mind.** An earlier limit deserves consideration, but an extension may be sensible. Do not define every override as failure. Investigate whether the consequence helps people make a decision they endorse.

**Streaks and pressure.** A streak for keeping limits may support a chosen goal; it may also encourage fixation or guilt. The fact that it rewards a different behaviour does not settle that question. Treat this as an experiment, including the ability to stop using it.

**Friendship and accountability.** Sharing a funny video and sharing one's usage history are different activities. Accountability should be investigated with attention to what is visible, who sees it, whether sharing is reversible and how leaving feels. Being optional at setup does not answer all of those questions.

**Useful reflection and surveillance.** A richer usage picture can help someone understand their habits while also exposing sensitive patterns. For a cross-ecosystem proposal, start with the smallest useful summary, explicit sharing choices, retention and deletion, shared-device handling and overlapping activity. Local processing can reduce exposure but does not by itself resolve coercion or access by someone else using the device.

**Information quality and personal choice.** A subscription feed or a recommendation from a friend can still carry false claims. Greater control cannot be used as evidence of reduced misinformation. Correction systems need their own investigation, including timing, errors, appeals and trust.

## The platform access argument

The useful policy contribution is precise: independent work needs ways to inspect platforms and ways to test alternative designs under appropriate conditions. Distinguish three activities: access to data, permission and capability to run an interface experiment, and operation of a public product. One does not automatically authorise the others.

The EU's DSA research framework is an established precedent for access to data concerning systemic risks; it does not establish a general entitlement to operate an alternative consumer interface. [European Commission explanation](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/news/new-measures-unlock-access-data-largest-online-platforms-support-research)

Keep a small capability register: the experiment, the content/data/player capability it needs, what is technically available, the permission relevant to it, the specific constraint encountered and what arrangement would permit testing. Separate technical absence, quota, cost, a policy interpretation, a refused request and an actual enforcement action.

Your concern about Villow's future is relevant, but a shutdown is not established or inevitable. YouTube's policies allow consideration of significant independent value while separately imposing player, advertising and data rules. This strategy review does not determine Villow's compliance. [YouTube Developer Policies](https://developers.google.com/youtube/terms/developer-policies)

Preserve the design knowledge through explanations and demonstrations using permitted material. That gives the work a useful life beyond any one implementation without turning a hypothetical shutdown into the project's organising story.

The Australian submission is a natural first policy publication: choosing a feed is one component of empowerment, alongside reflection, usable controls and stopping. Describe it as your submission and argument. Its presence in the attachments does not establish government endorsement or the details of its receipt. Keep a dated policy note separate from evergreen mission copy.

## Proposed website journey

Keep the existing wordmark, green identity and expressive spider. The current warm, playful character fits this direction. The strategic change is mainly the story, claims and destinations.

Use a short navigation such as **Our work · Design questions · Get involved**. About and contact can be reachable from the latter and the footer. Policy can begin as one substantive item within the work, rather than a large empty department.

The homepage should move through six connected sections:

1. **The ambition.** Keep the existing “The internet can be better. Let's fix it.” if it still feels right. Explain what “better” means immediately.
2. **A concrete starting point.** Introduce Villow with one compelling visual and three examples: a feed that ends, reflection that informs choices, and sharing or watching with friends. Link to the product site for the fuller tour.
3. **The questions behind the designs.** Explain why those examples matter and what remains to be learned. Show the wider concepts as proposals without presenting them as current features.
4. **How the work can lead to change.** Explain building, evaluation, publishing and platform adoption in a short passage. Place the invitation to researchers beside the actual questions.
5. **What makes experimentation possible.** Introduce the access issue and the Digital Duty of Care submission. Keep the detail in a dated brief.
6. **Useful ways to take part.** Try Villow and give feedback; help with a defined technical or design task; contribute research or policy expertise; introduce the work to a relevant audience.

Avoid empty results, regional, press or team sections. A direct explanation that Nicholas built Villow and is looking for collaborators is more informative than an organisational chart.

### Wording to test

Opening support copy:

> Digital services should help us do what matters to us, enjoy the time we spend, and stop when we choose. Fix the Web is an independent project exploring how to make that happen, starting with Villow: a different way to use YouTube.

Villow introduction:

> A feed that ends. A moment to ask whether a video was worth your time. Ways to share good videos and watch with friends. Villow brings these ideas into an experience people can try. Now we want to understand which choices help, who they help, and what needs to change.

Wider purpose:

> The ambition reaches beyond YouTube. Attention, relationships and trust are shaped in part by how digital services work. Villow gives us a practical place to start exploring better designs—and a way to make the questions concrete.

Access introduction:

> Building an alternative reveals questions that are hard to answer from the outside. We want independent developers and researchers to have practical ways to test different designs, with appropriate protection for users, creators and their data.

Invitation:

> Try Villow and tell us what helps or gets in the way. If you can contribute research, design, code or policy experience, help us turn the next questions into useful work.

These passages supply a direction for the next local preview. They are not a reason to replace the accepted visual identity or replicate Villow's whole homepage.

## What should happen to the older Fix the Web material

The existing development draft contains useful foundations, but some language conflicts with this direction.

- Replace “proof it can be better” with a description of what Villow demonstrates. A different interface is visible; better outcomes require evaluation.
- Preserve “Was this worth your time?” rather than substituting enjoyment or likes. Do not make reflection sound mandatory when the current preview describes it as optional.
- Separate current features from creator-action proposals. The existing homepage currently describes some of that proposal work as though it exists.
- Reconsider claims that country-level popularity gets people out of bubbles. The mechanism and outcome are different claims.
- Keep the Life calculator private while reviewing its philosophy and method. Its lost-life framing, preset definition of worthwhile activity and combined estimates could undermine a mission based on users' own judgments. It may become a bounded communication experiment, but should not define the launch.
- Keep the ordinary spider. Review Den progression separately: consequences that accrue during absence may create pressure to return. That is a design question, not a reason to remove play or the mascot.
- Replace the sprawling placeholder navigation with destinations that have useful content.

These are recommendations for later implementation. The production/development split and existing user work remain intact.

## A concrete next implementation scope

The first implementation should be a local homepage preview using the accepted identity, a compact Villow case study, a short access/submission brief and working contribution routes. The three initial design questions—finite feeds, worth-it reflection and subscription controls—can begin as sections of the case study. Keep social usability within the overall review of the experience. Support consequential factual and outcome claims, and distinguish existing features from proposed designs and untested effects. Keep the full setup, hosting, feature tour and YouTube-specific concepts on Villow.

Evaluate that preview through ordinary visitor tasks: can someone explain what Fix the Web wants, distinguish it from Villow, identify what exists versus what needs testing, and find a meaningful next action? Test the copy and hierarchy before expanding the page count.

The immediate deliverables are more useful than arbitrary effort percentages: one coherent explanation of the project, a small set of documented questions, a scoped evaluation plan, and one grounded access argument. New programmes should follow from evidence, collaborators and capacity.

Before eventual publication, verify actual product availability, final URLs and contact ownership; choose which version of the submission to publish; confirm feature descriptions against the appropriate implementation; and update time-sensitive policy claims. None of these requires changing the strategic direction or pretending that all future work is already underway.
